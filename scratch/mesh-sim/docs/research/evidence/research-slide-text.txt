Speaker notes:

> You do not need the LTE radio device unless you want it, you can just run with mmWave (mmWave channel model)
> But upper-layer stuff is reused, for example RLC
> RLC = Radio Link Control Layer, the layer between PDCP and MAC
> Segmentation and reassembly of packets
> Retransmission in ack mode
> Buffering before MAC scheduling
> PDCP = Packet Data Convergence Protocol, sits below IP
> Packet/header handling before radio transmission
> Sequence numbering
> Duplicate detection
> Handing packets down to RLC
> Roughly moves like IP/app  PDCP  RLC  MAC  PHY
> Link Layer is split into PDCP, RLC, MAC, major difference in Radio
> Mac vs. Physical Layer Refresh
> PHY: Deals with signals, SINR, beamforming, channel effects, interference, HARQ, transmit power
> MAC: Packet scheduling, resource allocation, how much data, coordination with RLC
> In simple terms MAC = scheduler/traffic controller and PHY = Radio hardware and air interference behavior
> RLC  Here is data to send MAC  Send it in this slot with these resources  PHY transmit it over the mmWave channel

### R02 slide 33

Slide text:

> Small-scale Fading Cont.
> How do we simulate that?
> Define a cluster (Channel Matrix ‘H’ = H1 + H2 + H3)
> Rays that arrive at roughly similar times/angles (20)
> E.g., Cluster 1 – Direct LOS, 2 – Building reflection, 3 – Ground bounce
> Generate per-cluster (Faster)
> Delay, Power, Cluster Angle
> Per-ray
> Angle (Cluster angle + offset), Phase (Random)

Speaker notes:

> Imagine an office building to the left of the UE
> Bunch of rays bounce off it at roughly the same time/angle
> That group of reflections is modeled as a cluster
> A cluster is just a physical object or surface in the environment that causes a group of related reflections
> The cluster center angle
> Where that object is located w.r.t the UE
> And then, each ray has a slight offset (lookup table)
> Each terrain has a different amount of clusters roughly (real world measurements)
> So, how many clusters and how spread out they are
> They define each cluster as having 20 rays
> They then build a channel matrix (H)
> For each (Tx, Rx, cluster) combination, sum the 20 rays
> Sometimes rays are constructive, other times destructive
> H
> A matrix of complex numbers
> Accounts for the multipath roughly
